# HDR to SDR tone mapping on the NVIDIA Shield

An Android TV application can tone map HDR to SDR on the development Shield, but not through Media3's built-in path and not from ten-bit samples. Media3's OpenGL tone mapper is unreachable on this driver in every released version, the decoder-side request key does not exist below API 31, and the hardware HEVC decoder hands back eight-bit frames in buffer mode. What remains is the route mpv takes: sample the decoder's output as an ordinary external texture, accept the driver's YUV to RGB conversion, and apply the PQ transfer function and the tone curve in a shader of your own. That route is specified behavior rather than a trick, but Kino would have to write the renderer, and whether the Tegra driver's conversion is correct enough to satisfy the playback contract has not been measured.

Facts labelled "measured on the development Shield" come from Kino's own device runs and from `adb` queries against `10.0.0.191:5555` while writing this note. Everything else carries a link to the source that owns it. Claims that rest only on forum posts are labelled anecdotal.

## Device identity

The development Shield reports Android 11, `SDK_INT` 30, `ro.product.model=SHIELD Android TV`, build `RQ1A.210105.003.7825230_4387.0822`, and `ro.board.platform=tegra`. Its GL driver reports `GL_RENDERER=NVIDIA Tegra` and `GL_VERSION=OpenGL ES 3.2 NVIDIA 495.00`, and `GL_EXT_YUV_target` is absent. Present and potentially relevant: `GL_EXT_texture_norm16`, `GL_EXT_color_buffer_half_float`, `GL_OES_EGL_image_external_essl3`, `GL_EXT_protected_textures`, `GL_EXT_EGL_image_storage`, `GL_EXT_external_buffer`, `GL_EXT_memory_object`, `GL_EXT_memory_object_fd`, and `EGL_EXT_gl_colorspace_bt2020_pq`. Measured on the development Shield.

The attached display reports `HdrCapabilities{mSupportedHdrTypes=[1, 2], mMaxLuminance=500.0, mMaxAverageLuminance=500.0, mMinLuminance=0.0}` and `colorMode 0` with `supportedColorModes [0, 5, 7, 9, 11]`, from `dumpsys display`. Types 1 and 2 are `HDR_TYPE_DOLBY_VISION` and `HDR_TYPE_HDR10` in [Display.HdrCapabilities](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/android11-release/core/java/android/view/Display.java), so this display advertises Dolby Vision and HDR10 but neither HLG nor HDR10+. That is a property of the panel currently attached, not of the Shield. Measured on the development Shield.

## Media3 playback-time tone mapping

Media3's OpenGL tone mapper lives in one shader, and that shader requires the missing extension. `DefaultShaderProgram.createWithExternalSampler` picks `fragment_shader_transformation_external_yuv_es3.glsl` whenever the input transfer is HDR, and that file opens with `#extension GL_EXT_YUV_target : require`. This is unchanged between the version Kino pins and the newest release: the shader is at [`libraries/effect/src/main/assets/shaders/`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.9.3/libraries/effect/src/main/assets/shaders/fragment_shader_transformation_external_yuv_es3.glsl) in 1.9.3 and moved to [`libraries/effect/src/main/res/raw/`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/effect/src/main/res/raw/fragment_shader_transformation_external_yuv_es3.glsl) by 1.11.0, with the same `require` line in both. [`DefaultShaderProgram`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/effect/src/main/java/androidx/media3/effect/DefaultShaderProgram.java) also raises "The EXT_YUV_target extension is required for HDR editing input." after compiling the program, which is why the observed failure surfaces as a shader compile error rather than that message. [1.11.0, published 2026-08-07, is the newest release](https://github.com/androidx/media/releases); Kino pins 1.9.3 in `apps/android-tv/app/build.gradle.kts`.

There is a second HDR sampling shader with no extension requirement. [`InputSwitcher`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/effect/src/main/java/androidx/media3/effect/InputSwitcher.java) routes `INPUT_TYPE_BITMAP` and `INPUT_TYPE_TEXTURE_ID` to `createWithInternalSampler`, which for HDR input selects [`fragment_shader_transformation_hdr_internal_es3.glsl`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/effect/src/main/res/raw/fragment_shader_transformation_hdr_internal_es3.glsl). That shader reads a plain `sampler2D` and carries the same `uApplyHdrToSdrToneMapping` uniform and the same BT.2020 to BT.709 tone curve. It is unreachable from playback: [`VideoSink`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/exoplayer/src/main/java/androidx/media3/exoplayer/video/VideoSink.java) declares `@IntDef({INPUT_TYPE_SURFACE, INPUT_TYPE_BITMAP})` and `MediaCodecVideoRenderer` registers `INPUT_TYPE_SURFACE`, so a MediaCodec video stream can only enter the graph through the external sampler. Feeding the internal sampler would require an RGB or RGBA16F texture that the app already holds, which is the problem this section is trying to solve.

The maintainer advice in [issue 826](https://github.com/androidx/media/issues/826) is still the only documented route, and it does not remove the extension requirement. dway123 recommends a custom `VideoFrameProcessor.Factory` that overrides `DefaultVideoFrameProcessor.Factory.create()` to force `outputColorInfo` to `COLOR_TRANSFER_GAMMA_2_2`, wired through a custom `MediaCodecVideoRenderer` and `RenderersFactory`, with a [worked gist](https://gist.github.com/dway123/934126c050482e9a2d3dcd43934b3730). That only changes the output colour; the input is still HDR, so `createWithExternalSampler` still selects the YUV target shader. dway123 links that same shader in the thread. The issue is closed as fixed.

Media3 itself treats the extension as the gate. [`GlUtil.isYuvTargetExtensionSupported()`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/common/src/main/java/androidx/media3/common/util/GlUtil.java) is a plain extension-string check, and [`HdrCapabilitiesUtil`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/test_utils/src/main/java/androidx/media3/test/utils/HdrCapabilitiesUtil.java) skips the whole OpenGL tone mapping test suite with "Device lacks YUV extension support." on such a device, alongside its other skip for API below 29. [`DefaultVideoFrameProcessor`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/effect/src/main/java/androidx/media3/effect/DefaultVideoFrameProcessor.java) states the requirement in its own javadoc: using HDR `Format#colorInfo` requires OpenGL ES 3.0 and the `EXT_YUV_target` extension. The public [tone mapping guide](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/transformer/tone-mapping) gives API 29 as the floor for `HDR_MODE_TONE_MAP_HDR_TO_SDR_USING_OPEN_GL` and does not mention the extension at all, which is why the failure looks like a device bug rather than a documented limit.

The remaining Media3 knob is worse than nothing. [`Composition`](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.0/libraries/transformer/src/main/java/androidx/media3/transformer/Composition.java) documents `HDR_MODE_EXPERIMENTAL_FORCE_INTERPRET_HDR_AS_SDR` as "Interpret HDR input as SDR, likely with a washed out look" and says outright that "HDR transfer functions and metadata will be ignored, contents will be displayed incorrectly." That is the output Kino's playback contract already forbids.

One structural detail matters for anything Kino might build on top. `DefaultShaderProgram.createWithSampler` sets `useHdr = isInputTransferHdr || isExpandingColorGamut`, and that flag decides whether the sampling program's output texture is `GL_RGBA16F` or eight-bit `GL_RGBA`. Lying to Media3 about the input colour to reach the SDR external sampler therefore also drops the intermediate to eight bits per channel before any custom `GlEffect` runs, and the SDR external shader applies its own SDR EOTF to whatever it sampled. Correcting PQ after that point is arithmetic on eight-bit values that have already been through the wrong inverse transfer function. A correct in-app tone mapper on this device cannot use `DefaultVideoFrameProcessor` at all; it needs its own `VideoFrameProcessor`, or it needs to bypass Media3's effect pipeline and render the decoder's `SurfaceTexture` directly.

Nothing in the 1.10 or 1.11 release notes changes any of this, and a repository-wide search for `EXT_YUV_target` at 1.11.0 returns only the shader, the two Java call sites above, and comments.

## The decoder-side request key

`MediaFormat.KEY_COLOR_TRANSFER_REQUEST` was added in API 31 and does not exist on the Shield. The [API 31 difference report](https://developer.android.com/sdk/api_diff/31/changes/android.media.MediaFormat) lists it among the fields added to `android.media.MediaFormat`, and the [Android 11 release branch of `MediaFormat.java`](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/android11-release/media/java/android/media/MediaFormat.java) defines only `KEY_COLOR_STANDARD`, `KEY_COLOR_TRANSFER`, `KEY_COLOR_RANGE` and `KEY_COLOR_FORMAT`. Media3 encodes the same fact, guarding every use with `SDK_INT >= 31` and the comment "MediaFormat#KEY_COLOR_TRANSFER_REQUEST is available from API 31" in `DefaultDecoderFactory`.

The documented detection pattern is configure-then-read-back. The [current AOSP javadoc](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/media/java/android/media/MediaFormat.java) says: "After configure, component's input format will contain this key to note whether the request is supported or not. If the value in the input format is the same as the requested value, the request is supported. The value is set to 0 if unsupported." So support is per-codec and discoverable only after configuration, never from `CodecCapabilities`.

Coverage is thin even where the key exists. Google's own [tone mapping guide](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/transformer/tone-mapping) says MediaCodec tone mapping is "Only supported on API 31+ on certain devices and on API 33+ for devices with HDR capture support", and `Composition` repeats that tone mapping is only guaranteed on API 33 and above on devices with HDR capture support.

There is no equivalent on API 30 and no NVIDIA-specific alternative that I could find. Not established: whether any vendor extension key on Tegra performs the same function. The Shield's codec configuration at `/vendor/odm/etc/media_codecs.xml` declares no vendor keys, only size, bitrate and feature limits; measured on the development Shield. That is not proof of absence, because vendor keys need not appear in that file, but nothing in NVIDIA's public developer material documents one.

## Getting ten-bit frames out of the Shield's decoder

In buffer mode, eight bits is a hard property of this decoder on this platform. `OMX.Nvidia.h265.decode` advertises only `COLOR_FormatYUV420Flexible` and `COLOR_FormatYUV420SemiPlanar`, and decoding a genuine HDR10 Main10 fixture with no output Surface yields `color-format=21`, `stride=640` at `width=640`, `getOutputImage().format` of `ImageFormat.YUV_420_888`, and a luma plane at exactly one byte per sample. The decoder still tags the output `color-standard=6`, `color-transfer=6` and attaches `hdr-static-info`, so the metadata survives while the precision does not. Measured on the development Shield.

The platform gave no way to ask for better until long after this device shipped. `COLOR_FormatYUVP010` was added to `MediaCodecInfo.CodecCapabilities` in [API 33](https://developer.android.com/sdk/api_diff/33/changes/android.media.MediaCodecInfo.CodecCapabilities), and `HardwareBuffer.YCBCR_P010` in [the same release](https://developer.android.com/sdk/api_diff/33/changes/android.hardware.HardwareBuffer). API 30 has no public constant for a ten-bit MediaCodec output buffer at all, so a Shield-era decoder had nothing standard to advertise even if the silicon could produce it.

FFmpeg confirms the same ceiling from the other side, which matters because it settles what libmpv would get. [`libavcodec/mediacodecdec_common.c`](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavcodec/mediacodecdec_common.c) maps MediaCodec colour formats to pixel formats through a table containing only `COLOR_FormatYUV420Planar`, `COLOR_FormatYUV420SemiPlanar` and four vendor eight-bit variants, all mapping to `AV_PIX_FMT_YUV420P` or `AV_PIX_FMT_NV12`. There is no P010 entry anywhere in the file. So mpv's `hwdec=mediacodec-copy` returns eight-bit frames on any Android device, not just this one.

The Surface path is a different question, and it is the one worth answering. Rendering to a Surface never round-trips through `CodecCapabilities.colorFormats`; the decoder writes a vendor-private graphics buffer that the compositor or the GPU consumes. Two things are clear about it on the Shield. First, ten bits reach the display, since NVIDIA documents [enabling Dolby Vision output for compatible displays](https://www.nvidia.com/en-eu/shield/support/shield-tv/4k-hdr-dolby-vision-display-setup/) and Kodi's `RendererMediaCodecSurface` switches the interface into HDR PQ when a PQ stream meets an HDR display, neither of which the eight-bit buffer path could support. Second, an app can bind that buffer as a GL texture, because [`OES_EGL_image_external`](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/OES/OES_EGL_image_external.txt) exists precisely to let implementations expose planar or interleaved YUV EGLImages as texture siblings.

What that texture yields when sampled is the decisive detail, and the specification is explicit: "Sampling an external texture will return an RGBA vector in the same colorspace as the source image. If the source image is stored in YUV (or some other basis) then the YUV values will be transformed to RGB values. (But these RGB values will be in the same colorspace as the original image. Colorspace here includes the linear or non-linear encoding of the samples.)" The transformation parameters "are taken from the EGLImage which is associated with the external texture." So for a BT.2020 PQ source the sampler is supposed to hand back BT.2020 PQ RGB, still non-linear, still in the source's colour space. An app that knows the stream is PQ can then apply the PQ EOTF, the BT.2020 to BT.709 matrix and a tone curve itself.

[`EXT_YUV_target`](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/EXT/EXT_YUV_target.txt) exists to skip that implicit step: its `__samplerExternal2DY2YEXT` "is used to sample a YUV texture image and output color value without any color conversion." Media3 wants raw YUV so it can apply a known matrix and a known EOTF rather than trusting the driver. That is a defensible design choice, not a hardware requirement. The extension's absence removes Media3's preferred path; it does not remove the ability to sample the frame.

Not established, and measurable on the device: what the Tegra driver actually does when a BT.2020 PQ ten-bit external image is sampled with a plain `samplerExternalOES`. Three things are open. Whether it applies the BT.2020 matrix or falls back to BT.601 or BT.709. Whether it preserves more than eight bits per channel through the sampler. Whether it silently applies a transfer conversion of its own, which the specification permits only insofar as the result stays in the source colour space. The experiment is small: decode the existing HDR10 fixture to a `SurfaceTexture`, sample it with `samplerExternalOES` into an `RGBA16F` framebuffer, read back known patch values with `glReadPixels`, and compare against the PQ code words the fixture encodes. A gate that reads pixels back from the live pipeline is exactly the kind of assertion this repository already prefers.

The other extensions the Shield exposes do not open a separate door. `GL_EXT_EGL_image_storage` and `GL_EXT_external_buffer` change how an image is bound, not what the decoder produces, and any `ImageReader` or `AHardwareBuffer` route still ends at an external texture with the same implicit conversion. `EGL_EXT_gl_colorspace_bt2020_pq` applies to EGL surface creation, which is how Media3's `GlUtil` builds a BT.2020 PQ window surface for HDR output; it is about getting HDR out, not about converting HDR down. `GL_EXT_texture_norm16` would let Kino store sixteen-bit unsigned samples, which helps only if ten-bit samples ever arrive.

Not established: whether NVIDIA ships any Android-side Tegra multimedia interface to applications on the Shield. The Tegra Multimedia API is documented for Jetson under Linux for Tegra, and I found no NVIDIA documentation offering an equivalent to third-party Android TV applications.

## What shipping apps actually do

**Kodi** does no application-side tone mapping on Android's hardware path, and its own developers say so. `RendererMediaCodec.cpp`, the renderer used when MediaCodec Surface is disabled, sets `m_renderMethod = RENDER_CUSTOM` and draws the external OES texture with the plain `SM_TEXTURE_RGBA_OES` GUI shader, with no tone mapping code in the file at all; `RendererMediaCodecSurface.cpp` only configures the GUI for HDR PQ when the display is already in HDR mode. Kodi's tone mappers live in `ToneMappers.cpp` and the `YUV2RGBShaderGLES` path, which the MediaCodec renderer overrides away. See [`RendererMediaCodec.cpp`](https://github.com/xbmc/xbmc/blob/master/xbmc/cores/VideoPlayer/VideoRenderers/HwDecRender/RendererMediaCodec.cpp) and [`DVDVideoCodecAndroidMediaCodec.cpp`](https://github.com/xbmc/xbmc/blob/master/xbmc/cores/VideoPlayer/DVDCodecs/Video/DVDVideoCodecAndroidMediaCodec.cpp), which sets colour standard, transfer, range and `KEY_HDR_STATIC_INFO` on the decoder and requests no conversion. In [the forum thread on exactly this complaint](https://forum.kodi.tv/showthread.php?tid=374021), Team-Kodi developer fritsch writes: "On Android, when using the HW rendering path, Kodi has no influence on the firmware doing or not doing any tone mapping, it is a black box." Team-Kodi member jogal adds that Kodi's own tone mapping on Android "only works with MediaCodec Surface disabled but... since Android devices are not powerful enough to handle 4K HDR video without hardware acceleration it is not really usable." Kodi's answer on the Shield is the firmware's answer.

**mpv and mpv-android** take the route this note identified above, and they are the only surveyed project that does. [`MPVView.kt`](https://github.com/mpv-android/mpv-android/blob/master/app/src/main/java/is/xyz/mpv/MPVView.kt) sets `hwdec` to `"mediacodec,mediacodec-copy"`, preferring the non-copy path, with `vo=gpu` or `gpu-next`. The non-copy path is [`hwdec_aimagereader.c`](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/blob/master/video/out/hwdec/hwdec_aimagereader.c): it takes the `AImageReader` `AHardwareBuffer`, imports it with `EGL_NATIVE_BUFFER_ANDROID` through `EGL_ANDROID_image_native_buffer`, binds it to `GL_TEXTURE_EXTERNAL_OES`, and declares the mapped image as `IMGFMT_RGB0` with `external_oes = true`. libplacebo then applies the stream's PQ EOTF and the tone curve to what the driver handed back. It requires only `GL_OES_EGL_image_external` or its ESSL3 variant, both of which the Shield has. The fallback, `mediacodec-copy`, goes through FFmpeg's eight-bit table above. mpv's own issue tracker records that this area is fragile: [issue 15498](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/issues/15498) reports `vo=gpu` with any `-copy` hwdec losing tone mapping in 0.39 while `gpu-next` was fine, [issue 10287](https://github.com/mpv-player/mpv/issues/10287) reports Dolby Vision metadata not surviving `mediacodec-copy` with maintainer haasn suspecting mediacodec itself, and [mpv-android issue 1088](https://github.com/mpv-android/mpv-android/issues/1088) reports green frames from `mediacodec-copy` on Amlogic TV boxes where the plain `mediacodec` path renders correctly. None of those reports is from a Shield.

**VLC and libVLC.** Not established. I could not find a primary source describing what VLC for Android does with HDR on a MediaCodec hardware path, and [VLC issue 26948](https://code.videolan.org/videolan/vlc/-/issues/26948) shows the Android HDR path has known gaps. Treat any claim that VLC tone maps correctly on the Shield as unverified.

**Stremio's own Android TV app** uses Media3 with libVLC as a fallback, so it inherits exactly the constraint this note describes. The [Tech Update #31 post](https://blog.stremio.com/stremio-tech-update-31-stremio-android-tv-updated/) records the app moving to "Media3 (ExoPlayer) version 1.4.0-alpha02" and automatically switching "to libVLC when Media3 (ExoPlayer) starts with audio only but no video frame was rendered". Stremio maintains its own forks of [`vlc-android-sdk`](https://github.com/Stremio/vlc-android-sdk) and [`vlc-android`](https://github.com/Stremio/vlc-android). Nothing in that post mentions HDR or tone mapping.

**Jellyfin's Android TV client** builds its playback on Media3 only. [`settings.gradle.kts`](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-androidtv/blob/master/settings.gradle.kts) includes `:playback:core`, `:playback:jellyfin`, `:playback:media3:exoplayer` and `:playback:media3:session`, with no libVLC or mpv module. Jellyfin's answer to HDR on an SDR client is the server: its [hardware acceleration documentation](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/intel/) describes HDR and Dolby Vision to SDR tone mapping as a server-side transcode using OpenCL or Intel VPP.

**Findroid** does no tone mapping and the resulting complaint is instructive. [Issue 862](https://github.com/jarnedemeulemeester/findroid/issues/862) reports a Dolby Vision profile 5 file with no HDR10 base layer showing "a purple/yellow tint" because Findroid direct-plays it while the official client has the server transcode and tone map. The workaround offered in the thread is Findroid's mpv backend with hardware decoding turned off, and the reporter's answer is that software decoding makes playback unwatchable. That is the exact trade Kino's playback contract already rules out.

**JellyPlay** and **Wholphin** both solve it by shipping a second engine. [JellyPlay](https://github.com/raulshma/jellyplay) offers ExoPlayer, mpv and LibVLC as switchable video engines; [Wholphin](https://github.com/damontecres/Wholphin) offers ExoPlayer or mpv. Not established: what either produces on a Shield from an HDR source, or which engine each selects by default for HDR.

**SmartTube** cannot do this at all: it vendors a fork of ExoPlayer 2.10.6 (`exoplayer-amzn-2.10.6/` in [the repository](https://github.com/yuliskov/SmartTube)), which predates the `media3-effect` frame processor entirely. A code search for tone mapping in that repository returns nothing.

**Plex** and **Emby** are server-side stories. Plex documents [HDR to SDR tone mapping](https://support.plex.tv/articles/hdr-to-sdr-tone-mapping/) as a Plex Media Server transcode feature with per-platform hardware requirements. Emby's equivalent originates in [issue 3480](https://github.com/MediaBrowser/Emby/issues/3480), also a server transcode. Neither is a client-side mechanism, and neither is available to Kino, which has no transcoding server.

So: of the surveyed applications, exactly one, mpv, does client-side HDR to SDR tone mapping on Android hardware-decoded video, and it does it by sampling the external texture and trusting the driver's implicit conversion. Every other project either leaves the conversion to the device firmware, pushes it to a server, or falls back to software decoding. Not established: whether mpv's result on a Shield specifically is correct. The strongest evidence is anecdotal, from the Kodi thread above, where the original reporter compares Kodi against "MPV player on nvidia shield tv" and judges mpv's result better. That is a user's eye, not a measurement.

## The Shield's own conversion

NVIDIA documents none of this. The [Color Space and HDR page](https://support-shield.nvidia.com/shield-tv-pro-user-guide/Color_Space_and_HDR.htm) covers only which colour space to select for HDR output and says the Shield picks one automatically; the [4K HDR and Dolby Vision setup page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-eu/shield/support/shield-tv/4k-hdr-dolby-vision-display-setup/) covers enabling Dolby Vision and cable quality. Neither describes what happens to HDR content when the output is SDR.

What is documented is that the controls are global user settings under Settings, Device Preferences, Display and Sound, Advanced display settings, and there is no application-facing API for them. `settings list global`, `secure` and `system` on the development Shield return no HDR-related keys at all, so an application cannot read the user's choice through the settings provider either. Measured on the development Shield. The device does report `vendor.sys.hwc.hdr.supported=1` and `vendor.sys.display-colorspace=YUV422-12`, both vendor properties with no documented contract.

Anecdotal, from the Kodi thread: a user testing on a Shield 2019 Pro reports that with Kodi's hardware acceleration left on, Dolby Vision disabled in the Shield's settings, "Match content colour space" enabled, dynamic range set to auto, "Display HDR content when available" disabled, and a custom display mode of "3840x2160 59.940 Hz RGB 8-bit Rec. 709", a Dolby Vision profile 8 file with an HDR10 base layer "didn't look washed out at all". That is one person, one clip, and a subjective judgement, but it is consistent with fritsch's description of firmware tone mapping as something that happens and that the application cannot influence.

Note that Android's own platform tone mapping is not this. [`libtonemap`, shared with SurfaceFlinger and the Hardware Composer, arrived in Android 13](https://source.android.com/docs/core/display/tone-mapping), three releases after the Shield's. Whatever the Shield does is NVIDIA's own composer and display pipeline, undocumented and unversioned.

Not established: the quality of the Shield's conversion, whether it varies by source format, and whether it happens in the composer or the display driver. Not established either: whether an application can observe that it happened. Nothing in the Android 11 API surface reports it.

## Dolby Vision

The Shield exposes Dolby Vision decoders to applications. `/vendor/odm/etc/media_codecs.xml` declares `OMX.Nvidia.DOVI.decode` and `OMX.Nvidia.DOVI.decode.secure` for `video/dolby-vision`, both up to 3840x2176 at 60 and both with `adaptive-playback`. Measured on the development Shield. The file declares no profile levels, so the component reports its supported Dolby Vision profiles programmatically through `CodecCapabilities.profileLevels`; I did not enumerate them.

Not established from a primary source: which Dolby Vision profiles the Shield decodes. The consistent third-party account is profiles 5 and 8 supported, profile 7 played as its HDR10 base layer with the enhancement layer ignored, and I found no NVIDIA page stating profile numbers. Kodi's own decoder logic is the closest thing to a technical corroboration: [`DVDVideoCodecAndroidMediaCodec.cpp`](https://github.com/xbmc/xbmc/blob/master/xbmc/cores/VideoPlayer/DVDCodecs/Video/DVDVideoCodecAndroidMediaCodec.cpp) treats profiles 7 and 8 as having an HDR10 fallback and deliberately routes them to the HEVC decoder rather than the Dolby Vision decoder when the display is not Dolby Vision capable, "to ensure HDR10 output". That is a general Android strategy, not a Shield-specific claim.

Application-side conversion to SDR is strictly harder than for HDR10, and no surveyed project does it on Android. Profile 5 has no HDR10 base layer at all, so ignoring the dynamic metadata leaves an IPTPQc2 signal that is not a valid BT.2020 PQ picture; that is what produces the purple and green casts in Findroid issue 862 and mpv issue 10287. Media3's `DefaultDecoderFactory` handles this by requesting `COLOR_TRANSFER_HLG` through `KEY_COLOR_TRANSFER_REQUEST` for Dolby Vision input, explicitly to "Ignore the dolby vision dynamic metadata", and that path is gated on API 31. On API 30 it is unavailable.

The practical answer for Kino is short. Profiles 7 and 8 carry an HDR10 base layer that can be decoded as HEVC Main10 and treated exactly like HDR10, inheriting all the constraints above. Profile 5 cannot be converted correctly by any mechanism available to an application on this device, so the playback contract's instruction to reject it stands on its own.

## Whether API 30 is a ceiling

Yes, as far as anything NVIDIA has said. Android 11 arrived with SHIELD Experience Upgrade 9.0, and [the current software update page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/shield/software-update/) lists 9.2.4 as the latest version with no newer Android base; [the 9.2 release notes](https://support-shield.nvidia.com/android-tv-release-notes/9.2/) mention no platform version change, HDR change or display pipeline change. NVIDIA has announced no Android 12 or later for SHIELD TV and no driver update exposing `GL_EXT_YUV_target`.

The 2019 hardware is now seven years old and the platform release six. Planning as though API 30 and driver 495.00 are permanent is the correct assumption. Kino's `ShieldHdrCapabilityTest` already asserts the absence of the extension and `SDK_INT < 31` so that a device or driver which gains either capability fails the suite, which is the right shape for a fact that could in principle change.

## What the sampler actually returns

The measurement this note called for has been run. `ShieldHdrSamplerTest` decodes `hdr-probe.mkv` to a `SurfaceTexture`, samples it with a plain `samplerExternalOES` into an `RGBA16F` framebuffer, and reads the pixels back. The fixture is generated by [`scripts/test-support/hdr-probe-fixture.mjs`](../../scripts/test-support/hdr-probe-fixture.mjs) from known ten-bit code words and encoded losslessly, so each value read back has an exact expected counterpart. Measured on the development Shield.

The driver does the right thing on all three counts.

It applies the BT.2020 non-constant-luminance matrix. Across eight chroma patches chosen so BT.2020, BT.709 and BT.601 disagree by far more than rounding, the worst channel error against the BT.2020 result is 0.0107, and the pattern of that error is a small constant offset rather than a coefficient mismatch. BT.709 and BT.601 are excluded by margins an order of magnitude larger.

It leaves the transfer function alone. A neutral ramp from luma 64 to 940 comes back linear in the code value, with a constant step of about 0.066 per sixteenth. Had the driver applied the PQ EOTF the ramp would be sharply curved. The frame arrives still PQ encoded, which is what a tone mapper wants, since it can then apply the EOTF itself.

It preserves ten bits. Sixteen luma steps one code apart come back as sixteen distinct values with a mean step of 0.001139, against the 0.001142 that one code in the 64 to 940 range represents. An eight-bit path quantises to 0.003906, which is 3.4 times coarser and would have collapsed those steps into groups. The limited-to-full range expansion is applied correctly.

So the conclusion the eight-bit buffer measurement seemed to support does not hold. That measurement described the ByteBuffer path only. On the Surface path the frame reaches an OpenGL shader de-matrixed, range-expanded, still PQ encoded and at full precision. `GL_EXT_YUV_target` is not required to obtain the pixels; Media3 requires it only to perform the matrix conversion itself.

## Weighing it

Three options, and the evidence does not split them evenly.

**Keep rejecting HDR on the TV client.** This is correct today and costs nothing. Media3 cannot tone map on this driver in any released version, the decoder-side request does not exist, and the alternative Media3 offers is documented by its own authors as producing incorrect output. Rejecting the source and returning to source selection is exactly what [ADR 0009](../adr/0009-render-video-as-sdr-without-software-decoding.md) and the playback contract prescribe. The cost is that HDR-only sources are unplayable on TV, which for a 4K-heavy catalogue is a real gap.

**Port the TV playback layer to libmpv,** the switch [ADR 0003](../adr/0003-use-separate-desktop-and-tv-stacks.md) reserved. This is the only surveyed mechanism that demonstrably tone maps hardware-decoded HDR on Android, and it would align the TV stack with the desktop one, which already runs libmpv. But the evidence is weaker than it first looks. mpv's working path is `hwdec=mediacodec` with `vo=gpu`, which samples the external texture and applies the tone curve to whatever the driver returned; it is not reading ten-bit YUV either. Its fallback, `mediacodec-copy`, provably gets eight-bit frames on the Shield, because FFmpeg's MediaCodec buffer path has no ten-bit mapping at all. mpv's own tracker carries active reports of tone mapping and colour breaking on exactly these paths, and none of the corroborating evidence for the Shield is anything better than one forum post. Porting is a large, mostly irreversible change to the TV player, its track handling, its shutdown sequence and its Skip Intro timing, bought on the strength of a mechanism Kino has not yet measured on its own hardware.

**Accept the firmware's conversion.** This is what Kodi does, and its developers describe it as a black box the application cannot influence. It fails the contract's own test on procedural grounds before any quality argument: Kino cannot verify what it did not do, so it could not gate it, and a gate is what makes a behaviour claim checkable here. The one anecdote suggesting the Shield's conversion looks acceptable also required the user to set five global display options by hand, which is not a state Kino can arrange or detect.

That measurement has now been taken, and it settles the choice. The numbers came back right, so a fourth option exists that none of the three above describes: keep Media3 and write the tone mapper. Kino decodes to a Surface as it already does, samples the external texture, and applies the PQ EOTF, a tone curve and the BT.2020 to BT.709 matrix in its own fragment shader against an `RGBA16F` intermediate. Hardware decoding is untouched, so [ADR 0009](../adr/0009-render-video-as-sdr-without-software-decoding.md) holds, and the result is Kino's own arithmetic, so it can be gated by reading pixels back rather than trusted. That is a far smaller change than replacing the player, and it removes the reason [ADR 0003](../adr/0003-use-separate-desktop-and-tv-stacks.md) reserved the libmpv switch for.

One structural trap is worth recording before that work starts. Reaching the SDR external sampler by telling `DefaultVideoFrameProcessor` the input is SDR also drops its intermediate textures to eight bits, because `useHdr` is what gates `GL_RGBA16F`. The precision measured above would be destroyed in the frame processor rather than in the driver. The tone mapper therefore must not route through `DefaultVideoFrameProcessor`.

## What this note could not establish

- Whether the same holds for HLG and for Dolby Vision. The measurement above covers HDR10 and PQ only.
- Whether ten-bit precision and correct matrix handling survive at 3840x2160 as they do at 640x360, and what the upload and sampling cost is at that size.
- Which Dolby Vision profiles `OMX.Nvidia.DOVI.decode` reports. The codec XML does not declare them and I did not enumerate `CodecCapabilities.profileLevels` on the device.
- Whether any NVIDIA vendor `MediaFormat` key or Tegra multimedia interface offers a ten-bit or tone-mapped output path to Android applications on the Shield. Nothing public documents one; that is not proof of absence.
- What VLC and libVLC do with HDR on Android's MediaCodec hardware path.
- What JellyPlay and Wholphin produce from an HDR source on a Shield, and which engine each selects by default.
- The quality of the Shield's own firmware conversion, and whether an application can detect that it occurred.
- Whether mpv's tone mapping is correct on a Shield specifically. The only supporting evidence found is a single forum comparison, which is anecdotal.
